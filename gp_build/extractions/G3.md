## Fluid-Dynamic Drag — bibkey: hoerner1965fluid — file: Hoerner_1965_Fluid-dynamic_drag.mmd
Bib: S. F. Hoerner, *Fluid-Dynamic Drag: Practical Information on Aerodynamic Drag and Hydrodynamic Resistance*, published by the author, Bakersfield CA, 1965 (2nd ed.). Chapter VI "Drag of Streamline Shapes"; Chapter I "General Information"; Chapter II "Skin-Friction Drag".

**Nomenclature (this source) — QUARANTINE:**
- `q = 0.5 ρ V²` dynamic pressure (Hoerner's convention throughout).
- `C_F` / `C_f` = skin-friction drag coefficient **on wetted area** (Hoerner writes `C_f`, `C_g`, `C_F` interchangeably in OCR; treat as flat-plate skin friction on wetted area).
- **`d/l` = thickness/fineness ratio = diameter ÷ length** (NOT aspect ratio). `l/d` = fineness ratio (=10 for our hull).
- `C_Dwet` = drag coeff on wetted area; `C_Ds` (`C_D∫`) on frontal area; `C_DV`/`C_DA` on volume^(2/3).
- `S_ψ = (volume)^{1/3}`; `C_pris` = prismatic coefficient.
- CLASH vs other sources: here `d/l` is a *shape* ratio; elsewhere `L/D` is fineness. Hoerner also uses `t/c` for 2-D sections (thickness/chord). Keep `d/l` and `L/D` straight.

### E-1 Drag definition  [block: hydro-drag | aero-drag]
LaTeX: $D = C_D\,q\,S = 0.5\,\rho\,V^2\,C_D\,S$
Context: master drag equation (Eq. 5). `S` = chosen reference area (frontal, wetted, or volume^2/3 — must match the `C_D` used).
GP-class: **monomial** (given `C_D`, ρ, S). Handling: **use-as-is**. Loc: Ch. I §5, p.TBD (mmd L900–902).
Notes: This is the drag block backbone. Pair with a `C_D` model below.

### E-2 Reynolds number  [block: hydro-drag]
LaTeX: $R_l = V\,l\,\rho/\mu = V\,l/\nu$
Context: Eq. 7. Ratio of dynamic to friction forces; `l` = characteristic length (hull length).
GP-class: **monomial**. Handling: **use-as-is**. Loc: Ch. I §6.3, p.TBD (mmd L920–922).

### E-3 ★ Body form factor on wetted area (THE Hoerner form factor)  [block: hydro-drag]
LaTeX: $\dfrac{C_{D\text{wet}}}{C_f} = 1 + 1.5\,(d/l)^{3/2} + 7\,(d/l)^{3}$
Context: **Eq. 28.** Total viscous drag of a rotationally-symmetric streamline body, referred to wetted area, as a multiple of flat-plate skin friction `C_f` at the same length-Re. So `(1+k) = 1 + 1.5(d/l)^{3/2} + 7(d/l)^3`.
- Term `1.5(d/l)^{3/2}` = supervelocity/average-dynamic-pressure increment (Eq. 26: `Δq_av/q = 1.5(d/l)^{3/2}`, since 3-D supervelocity ∝ (d/l)^{3/2}).
- Term `7(d/l)^3` = flow-separation/pressure component (Eq. 27), **negligible for d/l ≲ 0.2**.
- For our hull d/l=0.10: (1+k) = 1 + 1.5(0.0316) + 7(0.001) = **1 + 0.047 + 0.007 ≈ 1.054**. (Third term truly negligible; matches the project design notes's assumed form factor ~1.20 being conservative/appendage-inclusive.)
GP-class: **posynomial** in `d/l` (positive powers 1.5 and 3). Handling: **use-as-is** — already GP-legal as a posynomial factor multiplying `C_f`. Loc: Ch. VI §"Thickness Ratio", Eq. 28, p.TBD (mmd L2326).
Notes: **Crown-jewel equation for G3.** With `S = S_wet` this gives `C_D0,wet = C_f·[1 + 1.5(d/l)^{3/2} + 7(d/l)^3]`. Monomial-friendly because posynomial×`C_f` stays posynomial. Directly the "form factor as explicit function of fineness" the task wants.

### E-4 Wetted area of a streamline body  [block: hydro-drag]
LaTeX: $S_{\text{wet}} \approx (0.7\text{ to }0.8)\,l\,(\text{perimeter}) \;\xrightarrow{\text{body of rev.}}\; S_{\text{wet}} \approx 0.75\,\pi\,d\,l$
Context: Eq. 29 + surrounding text. Perimeter = max cross-section perimeter = `πd` for a body of revolution; constant 0.7 (fine shapes) → 0.8 (full/spheroid-like). Average 0.75.
GP-class: **monomial** (`S_wet = 0.75π·d·l`). Handling: **use-as-is**. Loc: Ch. VI §Applications, Eq. 29, p.TBD (mmd L2344–2350).
Notes: the project design notes uses `S_wet ≈ πdL·0.8 = 0.256 m²` — this is exactly Eq. 29 with the 0.8 (full) constant. Consistent.

### E-5 Wetted/frontal area ratio  [block: hydro-drag]
LaTeX: $\dfrac{S_{\text{wet}}}{S_{\text{frontal}}} = 3\,(l/d)$
Context: Eq. 30, using constant 0.75, circumference πd, frontal area πd²/4.
GP-class: **monomial**. Handling: **use-as-is** (convert between frontal- and wetted-area coefficients). Loc: Ch. VI Eq. 30, p.TBD (mmd L2350).

### E-6 Body drag on FRONTAL area  [block: hydro-drag]
LaTeX: $\dfrac{C_{D,\text{frontal}}}{C_f} = 3\,(l/d) + 4.5\,(d/l)^{1/2} + 21\,(d/l)^{2}$
Context: **Eq. 31.** Eq. 28 multiplied by E-5 (`S_wet/S_front = 3 l/d`). This is the coefficient the project's "C_D,frontal ≈ 0.10" refers to. Minimum at l/d ≈ 2.7 (d/l≈0.37).
GP-class: **posynomial** in `l/d` and `d/l` (mixed positive powers). Handling: **use-as-is**. Loc: Ch. VI Eq. 31, p.TBD (mmd L2354).
Notes: For our hull l/d=10: C_D,frontal/C_f = 30 + 1.42 + 0.21 ≈ 31.6. With turbulent `C_f≈0.003` → C_D,frontal≈0.095. **Independently reproduces the project design notes's C_D,frontal≈0.10.** ✓ Strongest cross-check in this file.

### E-7 Body drag on VOLUME^(2/3) + wetted/volume area  [block: hydro-drag]
LaTeX: $S_{\text{wet}}/\nabla^{2/3} = 4\,(l/d)^{1/3}$ (Eq. 33);
$\dfrac{C_{DV}}{C_f} = 4\,(l/d)^{1/3} + 6\,(d/l)^{7/6} + 24\,(d/l)^{2.7}$ (Eq. 34)
Context: volume-referenced drag (airship/submarine convention). OCR garbled exponents; reconstructed from Eq.28×Eq.33: `4(l/d)^{1/3}[1 + 1.5(d/l)^{3/2} + 7(d/l)^3] = 4(l/d)^{1/3} + 6(d/l)^{7/6} + 28(d/l)^{8/3}`. (Book prints 24 & 2.7; treat constants/exponents of 3rd term as approximate.)
GP-class: **posynomial** (approx). Handling: **fit→posynomial** if used (re-fit constants; OCR unreliable on 3rd term). Loc: Ch. VI Eq. 33–34, p.TBD (mmd L2365–2371).
Notes: **Theoretical optimum fineness for airship/submarine bodies: d/l = 0.25, i.e. l/d = 4** (flat optimum: only ~2% penalty over d/l = 0.2–0.3). Our l/d=10 is far past this — friction-dominated regime, which is the intended slender-torpedo design point.

### E-8 Volume of a streamline body  [block: geometry]
LaTeX: $\nabla = C_{\text{pris}}\,\lambda\,S_\psi$ ; $C_{\text{pris}}\approx0.6$ (fine fuselage) … $0.7$ (airship)
Context: Eq. 32; `λ` length, `S_ψ` = frontal-ish area term. (OCR ambiguous; the practical form is `∇ = C_pris·(π/4)d²·l` for a body with prismatic coefficient C_pris.)
GP-class: **monomial** (`∇ = C_pris·(π/4)·d²·l`). Handling: **use-as-is** (buoyancy/displacement constraint). Loc: Ch. VI Eq. 32, p.TBD (mmd L2359–2363).
Notes: Feeds the buoyancy ceiling (`m ≤ ρ∇`). For d=0.1, l=1.02, C_pris≈0.6 → ∇≈4.8 L (cf. the project design notes 6.25 L allowing ogive).

### E-9 Subcritical / laminar-regime body drag  [block: hydro-drag]
LaTeX: $C_{D\text{wet}} = C_{f,\text{lam}}\big[1 + (d/l)^{3/2}\big] + 0.11\,(d/l)^{2}$
Context: Eq. 24. Applies R_l ≈ 10⁵–10⁶ (laminar forebody + separated pressure drag `0.33 d/l` on frontal ≈ `0.11(d/l)²` on wetted). Not our operating Re (we are turbulent), keep for completeness / low-speed dive phase.
GP-class: **posynomial**. Handling: **drop** (out of Re range) or keep as low-Re branch. Loc: Ch. VI Eq. 24, p.TBD (mmd L2300).

## A Theoretical Study of Body Drag in Subcritical Axisymmetric Flow — bibkey: myring1976theoretical — file: document_compress (3).mmd
Bib: D. F. Myring, "A Theoretical Study of Body Drag in Subcritical Axisymmetric Flow", *Aeronautical Quarterly* 27(3), 1976, pp. 186–194. (RAE.) [Duplicate: document_compress (4).mmd — identical, ignore.]

**Nomenclature (this source):** `a` = nose length; `b` = parallel centre-section length; `d` = max diameter; `θ` = tail semi-angle (2θ = tail-tip included angle), radians; `n` = nose-index; `C_DA` = drag coefficient **based on body surface area**. Total body length normalised to 100 units, tail length = `100−a−b`.

### E-1 ★ Myring nose profile (modified semi-elliptical)  [block: geometry]

GP-class: **non-GP** (shape function, not an optimisation constraint). Handling: **use-as-is** for CAD/mesh generation; feeds wetted-area & volume integrals.
Loc: §3 Body Shapes, Eq. 8, p.TBD (mmd L71).

### E-2 ★ Myring tail profile (cubic)  [block: geometry]
LaTeX: $r = \tfrac{1}{2}d - \left[\dfrac{3d}{2c^{2}} - \dfrac{\tan\theta}{c}\right](x-a-b)^{2} + \left[\dfrac{d}{c^{3}} - \dfrac{\tan\theta}{c^{2}}\right](x-a-b)^{3}$, with $c = 100-a-b$ (tail length)
Context: Eq. 9. Cubic tail; `θ` = tail semi-angle. For large `θ` an ogive alternative (Eq. 10, below) avoids contour overshoot.
GP-class: **non-GP** (shape). Handling: **use-as-is** for CAD.
Loc: §3, Eq. 9, p.TBD (mmd L75).

### E-3 Myring ogive tail (alternative, large angle)  [block: geometry]
LaTeX: $r = \dfrac{d}{2}\left[1-\left(\dfrac{x-a-b}{100-a-b}\right)^{n}\right]$
Context: Eq. 10; used for the deliberately high-drag comparison body (θ=60°). GP-class: non-GP. Handling: use-as-is (optional). Loc: §4, Eq. 10, p.TBD (mmd L111).

**Key findings (not equations, but drive `C_D0` calibration):**
- Method = viscous-inviscid interaction (Hess–Smith source rings + integral BL, Ludwig–Tillmann turbulent skin-friction law). Transition fixed at **3% body length**; most results at **Re = 10⁷** (≈ our water Re).
- **Fineness ratio alone does NOT fix drag**: at fixed thickness ratio d/l=0.18, contour changes give up to **±10% ΔC_DA**. ⇒ the GP form-factor constant will carry ~10% CFD-calibration uncertainty; do not over-trust a single `(1+k)`.
- Optimum (min-drag) thickness ratio ≈ **0.18–0.20** (Young's value 0.20 confirmed). % drag reduction from streamlining shrinks as d/l→0.10.
- Reference area recommendation: body surface area, or `volume^{2/3}` for volume-limited designs.
Notes: This is the parametrisation everyone else (Gao, Vardhan, Renilson fore-body) reuses. **Cite as the origin of the Myring hull class.** No closed-form C_D → C_D comes from CFD; Myring supplies geometry + the ±10% shape-sensitivity caveat.

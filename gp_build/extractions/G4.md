# G4 — Added mass & buoyancy of a slender body of revolution / ellipsoid

Theme: Lamb added-mass coefficients (k₁ axial, k₂ lateral, k′ pitch) for a prolate spheroid vs fineness;
m_a = k·ρ·∇; axial vs transverse added mass; slender-body / strip-theory added mass per unit length;
buoyancy B = ρ_w g ∇; displaced volume; neutral-buoyancy; Fossen added-mass matrix M_A.

Bib: **J. N. Newman**, *Marine Hydrodynamics*, **40th anniversary edition**, The MIT Press, Cambridge MA,
**2018** (orig. 1977). ISBN 9780262534826 (pbk). LCCN 2017023272. Foreword by John Grue.

### Nomenclature (this source)
- `∇` (rendered `∀` / `\forall` / `ν` in OCR) = **displaced volume** of the body. (OCR frequently mangles ∇↔∀↔ν; also uses `ν` for kinematic viscosity elsewhere — disambiguate by context.)
- `ρ` = fluid density; `g` = gravity; `W` = body weight; `ρg∇` = buoyancy force.
- `m_{ij}` = **added-mass coefficient** (6×6, i,j=1..6). Indices: 1=surge(axial x₁), 2=sway, 3=heave(lateral), 4=roll, 5=pitch, 6=yaw. (Newman's x₁ = axis of revolution.)
- `m₁₁` = longitudinal/axial added mass; `m₂₂=m₃₃` = lateral (transverse) added mass; `m₅₅=m₆₆` = rotational (pitch/yaw) added moment of inertia.
- `a` = semi-length of spheroid axis; `b` = equatorial radius. `b/a` = beam-length (inverse fineness) ratio. Prolate ⇔ b/a<1; sphere ⇔ b/a=1; oblate ⇔ b/a>1.
- `S(x)`, `S′` = sectional area and its x-derivative; `W(x,t)` = local lateral velocity (Ch.7, clashes with weight W — Ch.7 usage).
- `M_{ij}` (eq 141) = **rigid-BODY** inertia matrix (mass/inertia, NOT added mass). `∀`→ displaced volume.
- `C_M` = apparent-mass (inertia) coefficient; `C_d`/`C_D` = damping/drag coeff; `l` = body length scale; `λ` = wavelength; `A` = wave amplitude.
- CLASH vs our canonical: our `δ` = wing overhang, Newman `δ_{ij}` = Kronecker delta. Our `k` = induced-drag factor; here Lamb's added-mass factor also called k — **rename Lamb's to `k_a` in the GP**.

### E-1  Buoyancy force & excess (net) weight   [block: buoyancy | entry]
LaTeX: $B=\rho g\,\nabla$ ;  net (excess) weight $=(W-\rho g\nabla)$ ; terminal: $(W-\rho g\nabla)=D$
Context: Buoyancy = ρg×displaced volume (Archimedes). For a freely falling/rising body the **excess weight
(W−ρg∇)** is the driving parameter; at terminal velocity it balances steady drag. ∇ = displaced volume.
GP-class: **monomial** | Handling: **use-as-is** | Loc: Ch.2 §2.4 (drag of a sphere), p.TBD (mmd line 304)
Notes: Core buoyancy relation for the vertical/buoyancy balance. `B=ρ_w g∇` is a clean monomial in ∇.
For the hull, ∇ is itself a monomial in geometry (spheroid ∇ = (4/3)π a b²; see E-6/E-7 geometry). The
"excess weight" is a **difference → posynomial-only if one side dominates**; in the GP use the buoyancy
*cap* form W ≤ ρ_w g ∇ (monomial inequality) rather than the signed difference.

### E-2  Neutral-buoyancy relation   [block: buoyancy]
LaTeX: $m=\rho\nabla$   (body mass = displaced mass of water)
Context: An unrestrained submerged (or floating) body in equilibrium is **neutrally buoyant**: body mass
m equals displaced water mass ρ∇. Used as the baseline for heave dynamics in waves.
GP-class: **monomial (equality)** | Handling: **use-as-is** (or relax to W ≤ ρ_w g∇ buoyancy cap) | Loc: Ch.2 §2.14, p.TBD (line 558)
Notes: This is the buoyancy-ceiling constraint already flagged in the project design notes (hull displaces ~6.4 kg at 1 m).
Monomial equality is GP-legal; for max-payload we use the inequality m_total ≤ ρ_w g∇ / g = ρ_w∇.

### E-3  Inertial (added-mass) force on an accelerating body — "virtual mass"   [block: entry]
LaTeX: $F=(m_{11}+\rho\nabla)\,\dot U$   (small body, l/λ≪1);  Morison: $F=(m_{11}+\rho\nabla)\dot U+\tfrac12\rho l^{2}C_{D}U|U|$
Context: Force on a body accelerating in fluid = **(added mass + displaced-fluid mass)×acceleration**. The
`ρ∇` term is the Froude–Krylov "buoyancy" part from the ambient pressure gradient; `m₁₁` is the added mass
proper. For a body accelerating in *still* fluid (our water-entry deceleration) only the added-mass term
`m₁₁` augments the structural mass: **effective axial mass = m_struct + m₁₁**.
GP-class: **posynomial** (sum of two mass terms × accel) | Handling: **use-as-is / algebraic** | Loc: Ch.2 §2.13, eqs (46)–(50), p.TBD (lines 534–548)
Notes: This is the hook for water-entry: the deceleration a = F/(m+m_a). In the a_peak fit
`a_peak=C·V^a d^b m^c`, replace m by (m+m₁₁) i.e. m_a enters the *effective mass*. Keep — it is why axial
added mass matters for entry g-loads.

### E-4  Ellipsoid — nonzero added-mass coefficients (general triaxial)   [block: hydro | entry]
LaTeX: only the six diagonal $m_{ii}$ ($i=j$) are nonzero (three planes of symmetry); depend on the two
shape ratios of the ellipsoid.
Context: For a general ellipsoid the added-mass matrix is diagonal (no cross-coupling). Values tabulated by
Kochin, Kibel & Roze (1964). Sets up the spheroid special case (E-5).
GP-class: non-GP (context) | Handling: **keep-context** | Loc: Ch.4 §4.14 "General Properties of the Added-Mass Coefficients" (p.147 per ToC), lines 1649
Notes: Confirms **no coupling** for our near-axisymmetric hull → we only need m₁₁ (axial), m₂₂=m₃₃ (lateral),
m₅₅=m₆₆ (pitch/yaw). Simplifies the M_A matrix to 3 distinct entries.

### E-5  ★ Prolate spheroid — the three added-mass coefficients vs fineness (Fig. 4.8)   [block: hydro | entry]  ← CORE
LaTeX (limits, from text — closed forms are in Fig 4.8 image, not OCR'd):
 - slender limit $b/a\to0$: axial coeff $m_{11}/\rho\nabla\to0$ (like a flat plate edgewise); lateral $m_{33}/\rho\nabla\to1.0$; pitch added-MoI coeff $\to1.0$.
 - sphere $b/a=1$: translational added mass $=\tfrac12\rho\nabla$ (coeff ½); rotational coeffs $\to0$.
 - $b/a>1$ oblate (flattened); $b/a\to\infty$ disc (renormalize).
Context: Spheroid = ellipsoid of revolution, axis x₁ = semilength a, equatorial radius b. **Three** nonzero
coefficients: longitudinal m₁₁, lateral m₂₂=m₃₃, rotational m₅₅=m₆₆, each a function of `b/a` only.
Nondimensionalized by displaced mass ρ∇ and mass-moment of inertia of displaced fluid.
GP-class: **non-GP as a curve** (tabulated) | Handling: **monomial FIT** (fit k_a vs fineness f=L/d=a/b via gpfit) | Loc: Ch.4 §4.15, **Figure 4.8**, lines 1651–1653
Notes: **This maps directly onto Lamb's k₁ (axial), k₂ (lateral), k′ (pitch).** Newman gives them as *graphs*
(Fig 4.8) + the asymptotes above; the **closed-form Lamb ellipsoid integrals (α₀,β₀ ⇒ k₁,k₂) are NOT in the
OCR text** — they live in the figure and in Lamb(1932) *Hydrodynamics* 6th ed §114 (cited by Newman, mmd
lines 1790/3481). ACTION for GP: our hull fineness f=L/d≈10 ⇒ b/a≈0.1 ⇒ read/fit **k₁≈0.02–0.05 (axial,
small), k₂≈0.9–0.96 (lateral), k′(pitch)≈0.9+**. Then `m_a = k_a·ρ∇` (E-9) is a **monomial** for fixed
fineness; if fineness becomes a GP variable, fit k_a(f)=C·f^p as a monomial. For our slender torpedo hull the
**lateral added mass ≈ full displaced mass (m₂₂≈ρ∇)** and the **axial added mass is small** — this is the
single most useful takeaway for the entry model.

### E-6  Slender-body strip theory — lateral added mass per unit length & integrals   [block: hydro | entry]
LaTeX: sectional force $F_x'=-(\partial_t-U\partial_x)[W(x,t)\,m_{33}(x)]$ (eq 39);
 total lateral added mass $m_{33}^{s}=\int_{l}m_{33}(x)\,dx$ (40);
 cross term $m_{35}^{s}=\int_{l}m_{33}(x)\,x\,dx$ (41);  yaw added-MoI $m_{55}^{s}=\int_{l}m_{33}(x)\,x^{2}\,dx$ (42).
Context: **Strip theory** (Lighthill 1960): each transverse section contributes its 2-D added mass with no
interaction between sections. For a circular section of radius R(x) the 2-D added mass of a circular
cylinder is `m_{33}(x)=ρπR(x)²` per unit length ⇒ integrating gives lateral added mass ≈ ρπ∫R²dx = ρ∇ (hence
k₂→1.0 in E-5). m₃₅ˢ=0 if body symmetric about x=0.
GP-class: **monomial** once R(x) fixed (∫R²dx, ∫R²x²dx are constants of the CAD planform) | Handling: **use-as-is / algebraic** | Loc: Ch.7 §7.2–7.3, eqs (39)–(42), lines 3658–3678
Notes: This is the "added mass per unit length" the theme asks for. For our **fixed planform** hull the three
integrals are just numbers computed once from the STL → feed as constants. m₅₅ˢ (pitch/yaw added MoI) needed
if entry pitch dynamics are modeled. `m_{33}(x)=ρπR²` = clean monomial in local radius.

### E-7  Longitudinal (axial) added mass of a slender spheroid — asymptotic   [block: hydro | entry]
LaTeX: $m_{11}=\tfrac{4}{3}\pi\rho\,\dfrac{b^{4}}{a}\Big[\log\tfrac{a}{b}+O(1)\Big]$   (as $b/a\to0$)
Context: Leading-order axial added mass of a slender prolate spheroid (length 2a, max diameter 2b). Shows
m₁₁ is **small** (∝ b⁴/a) relative to displaced mass ρ∇=(4/3)π ρ a b² — ratio ~ (b/a)²·log(a/b) → 0.
GP-class: **near-monomial × log** (posynomial-incompatible because of log) | Handling: **fit→monomial** (k₁ = m₁₁/ρ∇ = (b/a)²[log(a/b)+O(1)]; fit k₁(f)=C f^p over f∈[5,15]) | Loc: Ch.7 Problems, eq after (35), line 4154
Notes: Gives a *closed-form* for Lamb's k₁ in the slender limit — the one axial-added-mass formula actually
present in the OCR. For f=L/d≈10 (a/b≈10): k₁ ≈ (0.1)²·[ln10+O(1)] ≈ 0.01·(2.3+~1) ≈ 0.03 — consistent with
E-5. Use `m₁₁ = k₁·ρ∇` with k₁≈0.03 constant (fixed planform) OR the fitted monomial if fineness varies.

### E-9  Definition m_a = k_a·ρ∇ (added mass as fraction of displaced mass)   [block: hydro | entry]
LaTeX: $m_a = k_a\,\rho\,\nabla$   with $k_a\in\{k_1(\text{axial}),\,k_2(\text{lateral}),\,k'(\text{pitch})\}$
Context: The nondimensionalization Newman uses for Fig 4.8 (coefficients "in terms of the mass … of the
displaced volume of fluid"). k_a = m_a/(ρ∇). This is Lamb's convention.
GP-class: **monomial** (k_a const for fixed fineness) | Handling: **use-as-is**; if fineness variable → **fit k_a(f)** | Loc: Ch.4 §4.15, line 1653
Notes: The master relation for the GP. Combine with E-1: buoyancy B=ρ_w g∇ and added mass m_a=k_a ρ_w∇ share
the SAME ρ_w∇ factor ⇒ one geometry monomial drives both. Recommended v1: **k₁≈0.03, k₂≈0.95** constants.

### E-10  Rigid-BODY inertia matrix M_ij (6×6) — structure to mirror for M_A   [block: entry | method]
LaTeX: $M_{ij}=\begin{bmatrix} m&0&0&0&mz_G&-my_G\\ 0&m&0&-mz_G&0&mx_G\\ 0&0&m&my_G&-mx_G&0\\ 0&-mz_G&my_G&I_{11}&I_{12}&I_{13}\\ mz_G&0&-mx_G&I_{21}&I_{22}&I_{23}\\ -my_G&mx_G&0&I_{31}&I_{32}&I_{33}\end{bmatrix}$ (eq 141), with $m=\iiint\rho_B\,d\mathcal V$ (142)
Context: The **rigid-body** mass/inertia matrix (CG offset x_G,y_G,z_G). Newman's analog of Fossen's M_RB.
The **added-mass matrix M_A has the identical 6×6 slot structure** but populated by m_{ij} (E-4/E-5); for our
axisymmetric hull M_A ≈ diag(m₁₁, m₂₂, m₃₃, m₄₄≈0, m₅₅, m₆₆).
GP-class: non-GP (matrix) | Handling: **keep-context** (defines which added-mass entries the dynamics need) | Loc: Ch.4 §4.16, eqs (140)–(142), lines 1659–1669
Notes: Use to build the total inertia (M_RB+M_A) for the entry/trajectory 3-DOF sim (NOT inside the GP). For a
near-axisymmetric hull with CG on axis, off-diagonals ≈0 ⇒ effective masses along/across axis = m+m₁₁, m+m₂₂.

## Cross-source synthesis for the GP (G4 takeaways)

1. **Buoyancy (monomial, use-as-is):** `B = ρ_w g ∇`; hull buoyancy cap `m_total ≤ ρ_w ∇` (E-1,E-2). ∇ from
   CAD/STL, or analytic spheroid ∇=(4/3)π a b² (E-8). This is the buoyancy-ceiling constraint in the project design notes.
2. **Added mass = k_a·ρ_w·∇ (monomial, E-9):** for our slender hull (f=L/d≈10, b/a≈0.1):
   **axial k₁≈0.03** (small, E-7), **lateral k₂≈0.9–0.96** (≈full displaced mass, E-5/E-6), **pitch k′≈0.9+**.
   Constants for fixed planform → monomial; if fineness varies, fit k_a(f)=C·f^p via gpfit (monomial fit).
3. **Water-entry effective mass (E-3):** deceleration a = F/(m+m₁₁); axial added mass augments structural mass
   in the `a_peak=C·V^a d^b m^c` fit — replace m→(m+m₁₁). Because k₁ is small, m₁₁ is a ~3% correction axially;
   for a *belly-flop* (lateral) entry it would be ~95% (m₂₂≈ρ∇) — matters if entry attitude is not nose-first.
4. **Strip-theory added mass per unit length (E-6):** m₃₃(x)=ρπR(x)²; ∫ gives lateral m₂₂≈ρ∇ and pitch added
   MoI m₅₅ˢ=∫ρπR²x²dx — precompute from STL as constants.
5. **M_A matrix (E-4,E-10):** diagonal for our axisymmetric hull ⇒ only m₁₁, m₂₂=m₃₃, m₅₅=m₆₆ needed; feed
   (M_RB+M_A) into the 3-DOF entry/trajectory sim, NOT the GP.

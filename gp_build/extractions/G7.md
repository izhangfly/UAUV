# G7 — Energy, Endurance, Range & Electric-Propulsion Sizing

Theme G7 extraction for the HAUV GP sizing model. Target relations: E=P·t, R=V·t,
E=e·m_batt, P=D·V/η (efficiency chain), thruster efficiency, component mass-per-power,
hotel/payload power, propulsion mass, plus AUV endurance-range calibration numbers.

> Quarantine note: every source below has its own nomenclature block. Symbols are NOT shared
> across sources unless explicitly reconciled. Bib keys tagged (VERIFY) are unconfirmed.

## Autonomous Underwater Vehicle Design Considering Energy Source Selection and Hydrodynamics — bibkey: southampton — file: 1230769.mmd

Bib: Kieran Thomas Rutherford, 2008, *AUV Design Considering Energy Source Selection and
Hydrodynamics*, EngD thesis, University of Southampton (UK). **VERIFY** — title confirmed from
p.1 of OCR; this is a doctoral thesis, not the journal the task guessed. Grew out of NOCS/Autosub
programme; produced the pressure-tolerant Li-polymer cells later used on Autosub 6000.

**Nomenclature (this source):**
- `Spe` = specific energy of battery chemistry [Wh/kg]
- `SpeM_E` (written `SpeM_E`) = Spe × Mass_battery = total onboard energy [Wh] (their eq 3.5)
- `M_E` = mass of energy source (battery) [kg]
- `ρ_E` = energy gravimetric density = Spe / (Energy Volumetric Density) — a mass-per-volume [kg/dm³]
- `∇_S` (nabla_S) = displaced volume of the AUV hull [m³]  (∇_E battery vol, ∇_B buoyancy foam vol)
- `U_∞` = operating (cruise) velocity [m/s]
- `C_D∇` = **volumetric** drag coefficient (drag referenced to ∇_S^(2/3), NOT frontal area)
- `ρ_ψ` = fluid (water) density [kg/m³]
- `η_PT` = power-train efficiency, energy-source→water (**includes motor + propeller losses**)
- `P_Prop` = propulsion power; `P_Hotel` = onboard-systems (hotel) power; `P_Payload` = payload power
- `ρ_W`, `ρ_B` = density of water, of buoyancy foam
- CLASH: `∇` used for volume here vs `∇` (del) elsewhere; `η_PT` is a *lumped* efficiency, not the G7 chain η_motor·η_ctrl·η_prop.

### E-1 Onboard energy from specific energy × mass  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $Energy = Spe_{batt}\times M_{batt} = SpeM_E$   (eq 3.5)
Context: THE core battery-energy monomial the GP needs. Energy [Wh] = specific energy [Wh/kg] ×
battery mass [kg]. Identical in form to the task's target `E = e_batt·m_batt`.
Typical values (their Table 2.1, see E-7): Li-polymer Spe ≈ 190 Wh/kg.
GP-class: monomial | Handling: use-as-is | Loc: §3.2, p.~54, eq 3.5
Notes: this is the cleanest possible GP primitive. e_batt is a fixed coefficient.

### E-2 Battery volume from energy  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $\nabla_E = \dfrac{M_E}{\rho_E} = \dfrac{M_E\,Spe}{Energy\ Volumetric\ Density}$   (eq 3.14)
Context: converts battery mass→volume via volumetric energy density. Worked ex.: 550 kg batteries at
Spe=110 Wh/kg, vol. density 316 Wh/dm³ → ∇_E = 0.19 m³, needing 0.495 m³ foam (ρ_B=308 kg/m³) →
min hull volume 0.7 m³. Feeds the buoyancy/volume cap.
GP-class: monomial | Handling: use-as-is | Loc: §3.2, p.57, eq 3.14
Notes: ties battery mass to the volume/buoyancy budget — directly relevant to the HAUV buoyancy ceiling.

### E-3 Drag force (volumetric-coefficient form)  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $D = \tfrac12\,C_{D\nabla}\,\rho_\psi\,\nabla_S^{2/3}\,U_\infty^{2}$   (eq 3.7)
Context: drag referenced to volume^(2/3) instead of frontal area. **OCR CAVEAT:** the .mmd renders the
exponent as `∇_S^{2/3}` in 3.7 but drops it to `∇_S^2` in 3.8/3.9/3.10 — body text (p.55) confirms
"P_Prop decreases according to ∇_S^(2/3)", so the exponent is **2/3** throughout. Trust the text.
GP-class: monomial | Handling: use-as-is | Loc: §3.2, p.55, eq 3.7
Notes: alternative to frontal-area C_D·½ρV²A. For the HAUV keep frontal-area form (the project design notes uses A=πd²/4);
this is here for cross-check only.

### E-4 Propulsion power  [block: energy]  ★KEY
LaTeX: $P_{Prop} = \dfrac{D\cdot U_\infty}{\eta_{PT}} = \dfrac{C_{D\nabla}\,\rho_\psi\,\nabla_S^{2/3}\,U_\infty^{3}}{2\,\eta_{PT}}$   (eq 3.8)
Context: the task's target `P = D·V/η`. Power scales as **U³** and as ∇^(2/3). η_PT lumps motor+prop.
GP-class: monomial | Handling: use-as-is | Loc: §3.2, p.55, eq 3.8
Notes: the U³ law is the load-bearing scaling for the HAUV water sprint (20 m/s). Split η_PT into the
G7 chain (η_motor·η_ctrl·η_prop) when you have component numbers.

### E-5 Endurance (energy / total power)  [block: energy]  ★KEY
LaTeX: $Endurance = \dfrac{Energy}{Total\ Power} = \dfrac{SpeM_E}{\dfrac{C_{D\nabla}\rho_\psi\nabla_S^{2/3}U_\infty^{3}}{2\eta_{PT}} + P_{Hotel} + P_{Payload}}$   (eq 3.9)
Context: endurance t = E / (P_prop + P_hotel + P_payload). Denominator is a posynomial (sum of powers).
GP-class: non-GP as written (monomial ÷ posynomial) | Handling: algebraic-convert →
**write as posynomial constraint** `t·(P_prop + P_hotel + P_payload) ≤ e_batt·m_batt`, i.e. energy
balance E_stored ≥ P_total·t. That form is GP-legal (posynomial ≤ monomial). | Loc: §3.2, p.55, eq 3.9
Notes: this is exactly the per-phase energy balance the GP needs; do NOT keep the division form.

### E-6 Range (endurance × speed)  [block: energy]  ★KEY
LaTeX: $Range = \dfrac{SpeM_E\,U_\infty}{\dfrac{C_{D\nabla}\rho_\psi\nabla_S^{2/3}U_\infty^{3}}{2\eta_{PT}} + P_{Hotel} + P_{Payload}}$   (eq 3.10)
Context: R = t·V. Their derivative dR/dU (eq 3.11) gives an optimum cruise speed where
P_hotel+P_payload = C_D ρ ∇^(2/3) U³/η_PT (i.e. drag power = hotel power at range-optimal speed) — a useful
sanity rule. Worked base case: range peaks 357–743 km as P_hotel+payload varies ±50%; +50% energy → 703 km;
lowest C_D∇ gives 590 km and the highest optimal speed.
GP-class: non-GP as written | Handling: algebraic-convert → `R·(P_prop+P_hotel+P_payload) ≤ e_batt·m_batt·V`
(posynomial ≤ monomial, GP-legal) | Loc: §3.2, p.56, eq 3.10
Notes: the aerial 10–30 km @50 m/s and the water range requirement plug straight into this.

### E-7 Energy-source property table  [block: energy — CONSTANTS]

GP-class: n/a (constants) | Handling: pick e_batt | Loc: §2.3 Table 2.1, p.32
Notes: for a modern (2026) Li-ion pack use **cell 250–300 Wh/kg, system 100–180 Wh/kg** (see energiesreview E-3);

### E-8 Calibration data points (worked AUV numbers)  [block: energy — CONSTANTS]
- **Hugin II @2 m/s: 300 W propulsion + 220 W hotel**; with 65%-eff DC/DC converter → 800 W on the semi-fuel-cell.
- Hugin 3000: 48 h @ 900 W, 50 kWh, 472 kg → **system 105 Wh/kg** (100 Wh/kg incl. converter+NiCd).

## A Strategy for Sizing and Optimizing the Energy System on Long-Range AUVs — bibkey: fcauv — file: A_Strategy_for_Sizing_and_Optimizing_the.mmd

Bib: A. Chiche, G. Lindbergh, I. Stenius, C. Lagergren, 2021, *A Strategy for Sizing and Optimizing the
Energy System on Long-Range AUVs*, **IEEE J. Oceanic Eng. 46(4):1132-1143**. VERIFY (author list & vol/pp
confirmed from the paper's own ref [20] and byline). Journal extension of the 2018 workshop paper (= sizing2019).
KTH SMaRC; hybrid PEM fuel-cell / Li-battery sizing for Kongsberg HUGIN.

**Nomenclature (this source):**
- `P_fc` = fuel-cell output power (assumed **constant** over mission) [W]
- `P_max`, `P_base`, `P_average` = peak / base / mean mission power [W]
- `E_fc` = electric energy delivered by FC [Wh]; `Time` = mission duration
- `LHV_H2` = lower heating value of hydrogen; `η_fc` = overall FC system efficiency
- `m_H2`, `m_O2` = reactant masses; `M_H2`, `M_O2` = molar masses
- `Es_cycle` = battery energy sized for one peak cycle; `B_eff` = battery efficiency; `BU` = battery utilisation (SoC window)
- `DoH_Power` = degree of hybridisation (power); `P`=power ratio, `T`=time ratio; `t1`,`t2` = discharge/charge times
- `C_rate` = battery C-rate; `W`,`V` (in eq 16) = weight, volume of hybrid system / compensation; `ρ_comp` = compensator density
- CLASH: `P` = both power AND the dimensionless power ratio; `V` = volume here (velocity elsewhere); `T` = time ratio (not temperature).

### E-9 FC electric energy  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $E_{fc} = P_{fc}\times Time$   (eq 9)
Context: energy = constant power × time. The task's `E = P·t` in its purest form.
GP-class: monomial | Handling: use-as-is | Loc: §III-B, p.TBD, eq 9

### E-10 Hydrogen mass from energy & efficiency  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $m_{H_2} = \dfrac{E_{fc}}{LHV_{H_2}\times \eta_{fc}}$   (eq 10)
Context: reactant (fuel) mass ∝ energy / (heating value × efficiency). Directly analogous to a
"fuel mass" monomial if the HAUV ever carries a fuel cell. Uses LHV (produced water not fully liquid).
GP-class: monomial | Handling: use-as-is | Loc: §III-B, p.TBD, eq 10
Notes: **η_fc = 0.60** (overall FC system efficiency, assumed constant — their key constant, from ref [25]).

### E-11 Oxygen mass (stoichiometry)  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $m_{O_2} = \dfrac{m_{H_2}\,M_{O_2}}{M_{H_2}\times 2}$   (eq 11)
Context: closed-cycle underwater FC must carry its own O₂ (no ambient air) — a mass penalty land FCs avoid.
Numerically m_O2 = 8·m_H2 (32/(2·2)). Relevant only if fuel-cell option is on the table.
GP-class: monomial | Handling: use-as-is | Loc: §III-B, p.TBD, eq 11

### E-12 Battery sizing for one peak cycle  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $Es_{cycle} = \dfrac{(P_{max}-P_{fc})\,t_1}{B_{eff}\times BU}$   (eq 12)
Context: battery energy = (peak−FC power)×discharge time / (efficiency × utilisation).
Constants: **B_eff = 0.95** (battery efficiency), **BU = 0.80** (usable SoC window).
GP-class: signomial (has the difference P_max−P_fc) | Handling: SP, or algebraic-convert — if P_fc is a
fixed fraction of P_max the bracket collapses to a monomial (P_max·(1−f)); otherwise treat as an SP term or
introduce ΔP=P_max−P_fc as a variable with ΔP+P_fc=P_max (posynomial equality relaxation). | Loc: §III-C, p.TBD, eq 12
Notes: the 0.95 × 0.80 = 0.76 net battery-to-load factor is a clean derating constant to reuse.

### E-13 Degree of hybridisation & mission ratios  [block: energy]
LaTeX: $DoH_{Power}=\dfrac{P_{max}-P_{fc}}{P_{max}}$ (4); $P=\dfrac{P_{max}}{P_{base}}$ (5); $T=\dfrac{t_1}{t_2}$ (6); $P_{avg}=\dfrac{\int Power\,dt}{Time}$ (7)
Context: dimensionless mission descriptors for hybrid sizing. DoH involves a subtraction.
GP-class: DoH = signomial; P, T = monomial; P_avg = integral (non-GP) | Handling: mostly **drop** for a
battery-only HAUV; keep P=P_max/P_base and T as monomials if a hybrid split is modelled. | Loc: §III-A, p.TBD, eqs 4-7

### E-14 Neutral-buoyancy compensation  [block: energy/mass]
LaTeX: $\begin{cases}\dfrac{W_{hyb}+W_{comp}}{V_{hyb}+V_{comp}}=1\\[4pt]\dfrac{W_{comp}}{V_{comp}}=\rho_{comp}\end{cases}$   (eq 16)
Context: solves for ballast/foam (comp) to force system mean density = 1 kg/L. If ρ_system>1 add air/foam
(ρ=1.23 g/L); if <1 add steel (ρ=8 kg/L). Submarine rule: aim energy-system density ≈ 1 kg/L.
GP-class: signomial/algebraic (equality with sum in denominator) | Handling: algebraic-convert to the HAUV's
own buoyancy cap `m_total ≤ ρ_water·V_hull`; use this only to size ballast. | Loc: §III-D, p.TBD, eq 16
Notes: reinforces the project design notes's buoyancy ceiling. Their optimiser also enforces battery-charge-limited criterion
`DoH < 2T(P−1)/(P(T+1)²)` (eq 8) — a hybrid-only inequality, drop for battery-only.

### E-18 Non-linear weight amplification (qualitative law)  [block: energy]
Context: adding batteries → more mass → thicker pressure hull → more buoyancy foam → more displacement → more
wetted area → more drag → the added energy is partly cancelled by its own weight+drag penalty. A positive-feedback
loop (their Fig 2). Depth threshold ~2000 m where rigid hulls become inefficient; pressure-balanced (oil-filled)
design saves ~18.3% system weight at 6000 m but adds viscous drag.
GP-class: non-GP (described, not equated) | Handling: **keep as a caution** — it is why the buoyancy cap and the
mass→drag coupling must both be constraints, not post-checks. | Loc: §2, p.TBD
Notes: for the shallow HAUV (surface–10 m) the pressure-hull penalty is small; the amplification loop is milder
than the deep-sea AUVs this review targets — worth stating explicitly as a simplification.

Context: Zhao et al. (cited): AUV at **32 knots (~16.5 m/s) → power demand 1.11×10⁵ W (111 kW)**. Motivates
thermal/nuclear over batteries at high speed. Ammonia poly-generation system efficiency 38.6% theoretical /
16–17% realized (miniaturised).
GP-class: n/a | Handling: calibration anchor for the U³ power law at high speed | Loc: §4.3, p.TBD
Notes: sanity check — the HAUV's 20 m/s water sprint is in the tens-of-kW regime, consistent with the project design notes's

## Cross-source synthesis (for the GP writer)

**Canonical GP energy block (all monomial / posynomial-legal):**
1. `E_stored = e_batt · m_batt`                      (E-1) monomial
2. `P_prop = D·V / (η_motor·η_ctrl·η_prop)`           (E-4, E-20) monomial; D = ½ C_D ρ A V²
3. `P_total = P_prop + P_hotel + P_payload`           (E-5) posynomial
4. Energy balance per phase: `P_total · t_phase ≤ E_stored`   (E-5 rearranged) posynomial ≤ monomial
5. `R_phase = V_phase · t_phase`                      (E-6) monomial
6. Battery volume `V_batt = m_batt / ρ_E,vol`         (E-2) monomial → into buoyancy cap `m_total ≤ ρ_w·V_hull`

**Recommended constants:**
- Specific energy e_batt: **system-level Li-ion 100–180 Wh/kg** (E-17; use 150 Wh/kg nominal, 180 optimistic).
  Cell-level 250–300 Wh/kg only if you separately debit packaging. (southampton's 190 Wh/kg Li-poly is a fair pack floor.)
- Efficiency chain: **η_prop ≈ 0.81** (E-22), **η_motor ≈ 0.65 brushed / ~0.85+ brushless** (E-23),
  add η_ctrl/DC-DC ≈ 0.65–0.95 (E-8 Hugin used 65%). the project design notes's lumped 0.50 prop × 0.85 motor is consistent-to-pessimistic.

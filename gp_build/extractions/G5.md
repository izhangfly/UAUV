# G5 — Cavitation: σ, inception σ_i = −C_p,min, vapour pressure, supercavitation

Theme G5 for the HAUV GP sizing model. Underwater cruise speed is a swept variable (2–20 m/s).
Cavitation must be avoided at low/moderate speed (GP constraint **−C_p,min ≤ σ**) or understood
(supercavitation) at high speed.

**The single load-bearing GP constraint (this whole theme):**
`−C_p,min ≤ σ`  ⇔  `σ_i = −C_p,min`  ⇔  don't let the suction peak exceed the available
cavitation number. `−C_p,min` (a positive number, a fitted function of C_L or α) **comes from the
HYDRO CFD**, not from any of these papers. These sources supply the *definition* and the
*corrections* to that inequality.

## Cavitation and Bubble Dynamics (2014 Cambridge ed.) — bibkey: brennen2014cavitation — file: brennen-christopher-e-cavitation-and-bubble-dynamics-cambridge-univ.-press-2014.mmd
Bib: Christopher E. Brennen, *Cavitation and Bubble Dynamics*, Cambridge University Press, 2014
(orig. Oxford Univ. Press 1995). Ch. 1 §1.13–1.17 (inception), Ch. 8 (free-streamline / supercavity flows).

Nomenclature (this source):
- `C_p = (p − p∞)/(½ρU∞²)` = coefficient of pressure (Eq 1.13). `C_pmin` = its minimum over the
  flow field, **a negative number**; `−C_pmin` is positive.
- `σ` = cavitation number (Eq 14). `σ_i` = incipient cavitation number; `σ_d` = desinent (cavitation
  disappears on re-pressurising); `σ_c` = choked cavitation number.
- `p∞`, `U∞` = reference (upstream) pressure and velocity. `p_V(T∞)` = vapour pressure at reference temp.
- `ρ_L` = liquid density. `Δp_C` = tensile strength of the liquid (tension for nucleation).
- `q_c` = speed on the free (cavity) surface.
- CLASH: `σ` here = cavitation number; elsewhere in project docs `σ` = structural stress and `δ` = wing
  overhang. Quarantined. Also `d` in Brennen nomenclature = cavity half-width (not body diameter).

### E-1 Pressure coefficient  [block: cavitation]
LaTeX: $C_{p}(x_i)=\dfrac{p(x_i)-p_{\infty}}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U_{\infty}^{2}}$
Context: Definition of the pressure coefficient; independent of p∞ for fixed geometry. `C_pmin`
= min over the field, occurring at x*. In inviscid flow C_p depends only on geometry (Bernoulli);
with viscosity C_p and C_pmin become functions of Re.
GP-class: monomial (definition, once p−p∞ known) | Handling: algebraic-convert / definition-only.
Loc: Ch.1 §1.13, Eq (1.13), p.TBD (mmd line 350).
Notes: This is the *definition* underlying the whole theme. In practice `C_pmin(C_L)` or `C_pmin(α)`
is the fitted CFD output — a monomial/posynomial fit of the suction peak vs lift is what enters the GP.

### E-2 Cavitation number σ  [block: cavitation]  ★KEY
LaTeX: $\sigma=\dfrac{p_{\infty}-p_{V}(T_{\infty})}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_{L}U_{\infty}^{2}}$
Context: The fundamental cavitation number. Large σ ⇒ single-phase liquid; reducing σ (lower p∞ or
higher U∞) eventually reaches inception at σ = σ_i. With p∞ = p_atm + ρ g h (hydrostatic, see E-6),
σ = (p_atm + ρgh − p_V)/(½ρV²).
GP-class: posynomial in the numerator / **NOT a GP monomial as written** (difference p∞ − p_V in
numerator; and it appears as a lower bound so 1/σ is what the GP sees). | Handling: algebraic-convert.
Enter the constraint as `−C_pmin ≤ σ` i.e. `−C_pmin · ½ρV² ≤ p_atm + ρgh − p_V`. Rearranged:
`½ρV²·(−C_pmin) + p_V ≤ p_atm + ρgh`. LHS posynomial in V (and in the C_pmin fit), RHS posynomial in
depth h ⇒ **posynomial ≤ posynomial is signomial in general**; keep p_atm+ρgh as a fixed parameter
per depth (or a GP variable h) to keep it GP-compatible. This is THE speed-limiting constraint: it
caps V for a given depth and suction peak.
Loc: Ch.1 §1.13, Eq (14), p.TBD (mmd line 358).
Notes: σ ∝ 1/V² — the driver of the whole "cavitation caps cruise speed" story. At h=0, V=20 m/s,
p_V≈2.34 kPa: σ ≈ (101325−2340)/(½·1000·400) ≈ **0.495** (matches the project design notes "σ=0.50 at surface").

### E-3 Inception criterion σ_i = −C_p,min  [block: cavitation]  ★★KEY (the GP constraint)
LaTeX: $\sigma_{i}=-C_{pmin}$
Context: Ideal-flow inception: as σ falls, the first nucleus cavitates exactly when the local minimum
pressure reaches p_V, i.e. when σ = −C_pmin. For σ > −C_pmin the whole streamline stays above p_V
(no cavitation); for σ < −C_pmin a finite region sits below p_V. **This is exactly the GP
constraint −C_p,min ≤ σ.**
GP-class: monomial equality / inequality (once C_pmin is a fitted monomial of C_L or α) |
Handling: use-as-is as the cavitation-avoidance constraint `−C_pmin(C_L) ≤ σ`.
Loc: Ch.1 §1.13, Eq (15), p.TBD (mmd line 364).
Notes: **`−C_pmin` is the CFD deliverable** — fit `−C_pmin = f(C_L)` (or of α) from the HYDRO simpleFoam
sweep as a monomial/posynomial, then this becomes a hard GP constraint. Brennen lists 5 reasons the
real σ_i departs from −C_pmin (see E-4); for a *conservative* GP, `−C_pmin ≤ σ` is the right (safe)
inequality because tension/residence-time effects only *lower* σ_i (delay cavitation), while
gas/turbulence raise it. Use −C_pmin as the design ceiling.

### E-4 Tension / real-fluid correction to σ_i  [block: cavitation]
LaTeX: $\sigma_{i}=-C_{pmin}-\dfrac{\Delta p_{C}}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_{L}U_{\infty}^{2}}$
Context: In a degassed liquid nucleation needs a positive tension Δp_C, lowering σ_i below −C_pmin.
Five listed reasons σ_i ≠ −C_pmin: (1) tensile strength lowers σ_i; (2) residence-time lowers σ_i;
(3) contaminant gas raises σ_i; (4) steady viscous effect: C_pmin = C_pmin(Re); (5) turbulence
(vortex-core pressure) raises σ_i.
GP-class: signomial (subtraction) | Handling: drop for v1 (use E-3 conservatively); note as an
uncertainty band on the constraint. The Δp_C term is a fixed offset that *relaxes* the constraint.
Loc: Ch.1 §1.13, p.TBD (mmd line 370); reasons list mmd lines 378–386.
Notes: Real seawater has abundant nuclei ⇒ σ_i ≈ −C_pmin is the practical (slightly conservative)
choice. Keep E-3 as the constraint; treat E-4 as justification that E-3 is safe.

### E-5 Supercavity free-surface speed (high-speed regime)  [block: cavitation]
LaTeX: $q_{c}=U_{\infty}(1+\sigma)^{\frac{1}{2}}$
Context: On a fully-developed cavity surface (constant cavity pressure p_c ⇒ constant σ), the
tangential speed is uniform, q_c = U∞(1+σ)^½ (Bernoulli with cavity pressure). Basis of free-
streamline / supercavitation theory (Ch. 8). Underpins the classic supercavity drag law
C_D = C_D0(1+σ) (see Zou/Dzielski below).
GP-class: posynomial-ish (½ power of (1+σ)) | Handling: keep for high-speed context; SP/fit if a
supercavitating branch is ever modelled. Not needed for the cavitation-*avoidance* GP.
Loc: Ch.8 §8.x, p.TBD (mmd line 2554).
Notes: For the HAUV, the 2–20 m/s sweep is *sub*-cavitating; supercavitation only matters if a
high-speed sprint branch is added. Flag, don't use in v1.

### E-6 Hydrostatic reference pressure (context, standard)  [block: cavitation]
LaTeX: $p_{\infty}=p_{atm}+\rho g h$
Context: Not stated as a numbered Brennen equation but is the p∞ that enters σ (E-2). Depth h raises
p∞ ⇒ raises σ ⇒ suppresses cavitation. the project design notes: σ = 0.50 at surface, 0.99 at 10 m for V=20 m/s —
consistent with adding ρg·10 ≈ 98 kPa (doubles the numerator).
GP-class: posynomial (monomial in h if p_atm folded in) | Handling: use-as-is; treat h as a fixed
operating depth or a GP variable.
Loc: standard hydrostatics; used implicitly throughout Brennen Ch.1.
Notes: Deeper = safer against cavitation. The GP can trade cruise depth against cruise speed via E-2.

### E-7 Vapour pressure of water p_V(T) — values  [block: cavitation]  ★KEY DATA
Context: p_V is the temperature-dependent saturated vapour pressure of water; it sets the numerator of
σ (E-2). Brennen does not tabulate p_V(T) explicitly in the OCR'd sections, but gives liquid
properties at reference temperatures used in bubble dynamics. **Standard values to USE** (external,
standard steam tables — Brennen assumes these):
- 5 °C: p_V ≈ 0.87 kPa · 10 °C: ≈ 1.23 kPa · 15 °C: ≈ 1.71 kPa · **20 °C: ≈ 2.34 kPa**
  · 25 °C: ≈ 3.17 kPa · 30 °C: ≈ 4.25 kPa.
GP-class: fixed parameter | Handling: use-as-is as a constant (pick seawater temp, e.g. 15 °C ⇒
1.7 kPa, or 20 °C ⇒ 2.34 kPa). Negligible vs p_atm=101 kPa at the surface but NOT negligible in σ's
sensitivity at low speed.
Loc: Brennen gives water properties e.g. at 300 K (S=0.0717, μ_L=8.63e-4, ρ_L=996.3) mmd line 1210;
temperature dependence of p_V is implied not tabulated. p.TBD.
Notes: For the GP, p_V is a constant ~2 kPa; the temperature choice barely moves the surface-level σ
but matters if the vehicle operates warm/shallow at low V. Cite standard steam-table value, flag that
Brennen's own p_V(T) table is not in the OCR (gap — see below).

## Correlation between Pressure Minima and Cavitation Inception Numbers — bibkey: cavinception (VERIFIED) — file: jmse-10-00871-with-cover.mmd
Bib: Eduard Amromin & Kirill Rozhdestvensky, "Correlation between Pressure Minima and Cavitation
Inception Numbers: Fundamentals and Hydrofoil Flows", *J. Marine Sci. Eng.* **2022**, 10(7), 871.
DOI 10.3390/jmse10070871. **VERIFIED**: matches the assigned "J. Marine Sci. Eng. 2022, pressure minima
↔ inception" descriptor exactly.

Nomenclature (this source):
- `σ_i` = cavitation inception number (max cavitation number giving a steady cavity of decreasing size).
- `σ_n = 2(P0−Pc)/(ρ n²D²)` = *propeller* inception number (n=rev freq, D=prop diameter). Not used for us.
- `Cp = 1 − U²` (normalised local speed U); `min{Cp}` its minimum ⇒ `−min{Cp}` the suction peak.
- `D_σ = |min{Cp}| − σ_i` = the (Re-dependent) gap between the naive criterion and true inception.
- `P_V` vapour pressure; `σ_vapor` (vapour cavitation number) vs actual σ; `dσ = σ_vapor − σ` correction.
- `Re_s` local Reynolds number. CLASH: δ here = hydrofoil thickness (not wing overhang); quarantined.

### E-8 Cavitation inception number (definition, hydrofoil form)  [block: cavitation]  ★KEY
LaTeX: $\sigma_{i}(Re)=\max\!\left\{\dfrac{2(P_{0}-P_{c})}{\rho V_{a}^{2}}\right\}$
Context: Inception number as the maximum cavitation number over steady shrinking cavities; P0=inflow
pressure, Pc=cavity pressure, Va=inflow speed. Reduces to σ (E-2) when Pc→p_V. This is the paper's
operational definition, contrasted with the "archaic" −min{Cp}.
GP-class: non-GP (max operator, from CFD/experiment) | Handling: this IS the fitted quantity; comes
from CFD/MZM, feeds the GP as −C_pmin (or a corrected value). 
Loc: §1 Introduction, p.TBD (mmd line 35).
Notes: The whole paper's thesis: **σ_i ≠ −min{Cp} in general** — the naive criterion (E-3) is only the
leading term. See E-9.

### E-9 Correction: inception gap D_σ, and its Re-collapse  [block: cavitation]  ★★KEY CAVEAT
LaTeX: $D_{\sigma}=\big|\,\min\{C_p\}\,\big|-\sigma_{i}$ ; $\;D_\sigma \to 0$ as $Re_s \gtrsim 10^{7}$
Context: The archaic view (inception at pressure-minimum = vapour pressure, i.e. σ_i = −min{Cp}) was
refuted: viscous/leading-edge effects make σ_i < |min{Cp}| by D_σ. Crucially **D_σ becomes negligible
at local Re ≳ 10⁷** (their Fig. 2, ref [4]). D_σ grows with angle of attack / C_L/δ ratio.
GP-class: signomial (subtraction) / fitted | Handling: drop for v1 but **KEEP AS JUSTIFICATION** that
σ_i ≈ −C_pmin is acceptable for the HAUV: our hull Re = 2.0×10⁷ (the project design notes §3), comfortably above the
10⁷ where D_σ → 0. So E-3 (`−C_pmin ≤ σ`) is well-justified at our scale, and is *conservative*
(σ_i ≤ |min Cp|, so requiring σ ≥ −C_pmin is safe).
Loc: §1 Introduction (mmd line 35) + Fig. 2 (mmd line 43); "negligible at Re_s ~10⁷".
Notes: This is the most valuable single takeaway from this paper for the GP: it validates using the
CFD-fitted −C_pmin directly as σ_i at our Reynolds number, and warns it becomes non-conservative
(over-predicts safe speed) at low Re / high α on small appendages.

### E-10 Pressure coefficient along a parabolic (rounded LE) contour  [block: cavitation]

Context: Asymptotic pressure distribution near a rounded leading edge (r_lc=LE radius, C=chord,

GP-class: non-GP (analytic, has ±√) | Handling: drop (specific to hydrofoil LE); relevant only if we

### E-11 Vapour-cavitation correction dσ (gas-content)  [block: cavitation]
LaTeX: $d\sigma=\dfrac{2P_{V}}{\rho_{w}U_{0}^{2}}\Big[1-\Omega-m\big(\tfrac{\rho\Lambda^{1-1/\kappa}}{\rho_{at}}(\tfrac{P_{at}}{P_{V}})^{1/\kappa}-\Omega\big)\Big]$,  $\Omega=1+b(\lambda-1)^{\kappa}$, $\kappa=1.4$, $\lambda=P_0/P_V$

## Cross-source summary (the GP-relevant core)
1. **σ = (p_atm + ρgh − p_V)/(½ρV²)** (Brennen E-2) — cavitation number; σ ∝ 1/V², rises with depth.
2. **σ_i = −C_p,min** (Brennen E-3) — inception criterion = the GP constraint `−C_p,min ≤ σ`.
   `−C_p,min` is the **CFD deliverable** (fit vs C_L or α from HYDRO simpleFoam).
3. **C_p = (p−p∞)/(½ρU∞²)** (Brennen E-1) — definition; C_pmin is the suction peak.
4. **p_V(20 °C) ≈ 2.34 kPa** (E-7, standard steam table) — a fixed ~2 kPa parameter.
5. **D_σ = |minCp| − σ_i → 0 at Re ≳ 10⁷** (Amromin E-9) — validates σ_i ≈ −C_pmin at our hull
   Re = 2×10⁷, and shows it is conservative. Highest-value caveat.
6. High-speed bracket only: **C_D = C_x0(1+σ), C_x0 = 0.82** (Logvinovich 1972, confirmed from
   Dzielski PDF E-14; also Zou E-12, Brennen Ch.8 E-5 q_c=U∞(1+σ)^½).
